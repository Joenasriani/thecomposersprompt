Chapter 33: Advanced Scales, Modes & Computational Composition

Advanced extension · A as a common reference pitch. This chapter extends the preceding curriculum without replacing its tonal and compositional foundations. A is used as the common reference to make interval comparisons visible. For symmetrical or chromatic collections, starting on A does not itself establish an audible A tonic. Selected catalog examples are not exhaustive accounts of world musical systems.

33.1 Numerical scale degrees and interval notation

Degrees use 1, ♭2, 2, ♯2, ♭3, 3, 4, ♯4, ♭5, 5, ♯5, ♭6, 6, ♭7, 7 relative to a major-scale reference. A major: A B C♯ D E F♯ G♯. Lowered 3 = C natural; raised 4 = D♯. Roman numerals label chords/function, not scale degrees. Enharmonic spellings depend on analytical context.

33.6 Modes of limited transposition

A mode of limited transposition returns to the same pitch-class collection after fewer than twelve distinct chromatic transpositions. Whole-tone and octatonic collections are examples; Olivier Messiaen catalogued seven such modes. Check pitch-class sets rather than relying on scale names.

33.8 Beyond twelve-tone Western scale lists: maqam and raga

An Arabic maqam includes characteristic melodic pathways, ajnas, modulation, ornamentation, tonal emphasis and intonation. Seven equal-tempered piano keys do not fully specify its sayr. A tradition-sensitive study needs authoritative recordings and sources; microtones may not be playable on this equal-tempered reference keyboard.

An Indian raga is not just a Western scale transposed to A. Ascent/descent, recurring phrases, emphasized tones, ornamentation, register and context are integral. Naming a maqam or raga in a prompt cannot guarantee culturally or technically faithful output.

33.9 Comparative A-reference studies

  • A Phrygian dominant vs A double harmonic major: identical A B♭ C♯ D E F, differ in seventh G vs G♯.
  • A Dorian vs A Aeolian: identical A B C D E G, differ in sixth F♯ vs F.
  • A whole tone vs octatonic half–whole: six-note whole-step symmetry versus eight-note alternating semitones; verify exact intended pitch classes.

33.10 From scale formula to a musical prompt

Specify reference pitch, exact pitch collection, characteristic interval, register, bass/drone role, phrase destination, instrumentation and evidence standard. Scale names do not independently specify note order, rhythm, authenticity or model compliance.

Copy-ready prompt 01 · A double harmonic major

Compose a short instrumental passage centered on A using A, B-flat, C-sharp, D, E, F and G-sharp. Keep an A bass pedal and emphasize B-flat to C-sharp and G-sharp to A in the principal melody. End with a clear melodic arrival on A. Use a restrained arrangement so the specified pitches can be checked.

Copy-ready prompt 02 · A Dorian versus A Aeolian

Create two short instrumental variations centered on A, with the same rhythmic outline, tempo and instrumentation. For version one, use A Dorian: A B C D E F-sharp G. For version two, use A Aeolian: A B C D E F G. Emphasize the sixth degree in both versions. State which versions are requests rather than verified outcomes.

Copy-ready prompt 03 · A whole-tone collection

Create a short, sparse instrumental texture using the A whole-tone collection A B C-sharp D-sharp F G. Sustain A as a reference tone but avoid claiming a conventional major/minor tonic. Use a recurring ascending whole-step figure, then compare the recorded output with the requested pitch collection.

Copy-ready prompt 04 · A Phrygian dominant comparison

Compose two short A-centered melodies. In both, use A B-flat C-sharp D E F and a restrained drone on A. In the first, add G natural (A Phrygian dominant); in the second, use G-sharp (A double harmonic major). Preserve the intended melodic contour and inspect the seventh degree in each rendering.

33.11 Guided advanced exercises

  • Compare A harmonic minor and A Phrygian dominant. Identify changes in degrees 2, 3 and 7; control other factors.
  • Contrast A Lydian dominant with A Mixolydian, isolating the altered fourth.
  • Compare A minor pentatonic with the minor blues collection, adding E♭ without asserting that pitch lists capture blues phrasing.
  • Contrast whole tone with octatonic half–whole; state cardinality, interval pattern and verification method.
  • Explain why maqam/raga authenticity requires tradition-specific references beyond a prompt and equal-tempered note list.
  • Choose a verified heptatonic scale; define a four-bar intention, copy-ready prompt and verification rubric.

33.12 Chapter summary and final prompting module

Scale-degree accidentals modify a major-scale reference, but collections have different purposes. Reference pitch does not imply tonic. Distinguish intervallic structure, transpositional symmetry, melodic emphasis and tradition-specific practices. A written prompt is a hypothesis until audio or transcription is checked.

Final scale study

Plan a sixteen-bar instrumental scale study with A as the sustained bass reference. In bars 1–8, use A Dorian (A B C D E F-sharp G) and make F-sharp audible as its characteristic natural sixth. In bars 9–16, change to A Aeolian (A B C D E F G) and make F natural audible in a related melody. Keep tempo, meter and instrumentation consistent. Produce a written bar plan before generation; afterwards, report which pitch differences are verified and which remain uncertain.

Prompt language: the bar spans specify length; A is a bass reference, not a claimed cadential key. The two collections differ at degree six. Verification requires audible or transcribed evidence.

Guided variations: compare Phrygian dominant with double harmonic major; compare diatonic with whole tone; or encode the intended notes in notation/MIDI for precise control.
