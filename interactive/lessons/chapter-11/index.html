Chapter 11: Function, Cadence, and the Section

11.1 The T–S–D Grouping

Western harmony groups chords into three jobs. This is the foundational table:

T - S - D - T — the idealized phrase

Function Chords Role
Tonic (T) I, vi, iii Home, stability, rest
Subdominant / Predominant (S) IV, ii Departure, moves away from home
Dominant (D) V, vii° Tension, strongest drive to resolve to I

Functional harmony is "a system where chords serve specific roles in establishing resolution and movement within a key, primarily categorized as tonic (stability), dominant (tension), and subdominant (preparation)."

Why this matters for AI: AI doesn't follow function, it observes frequency. A true ii–V–I implies stepwise inner voices, bass moving by descending fifths, and tritones resolving—not just labels. When you hear AI produce D♭7 → G♭maj7 where C♭ should resolve to B♭ but doesn't, you need functional ears to catch it.
