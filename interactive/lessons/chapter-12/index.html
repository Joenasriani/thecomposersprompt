Chapter 12: Non-Chord Tones — The Life of the Line

12.1 A Taxonomy of Melodic Motion

Non-chord tones (NCTs) are pitches that are not members of the prevailing harmony.

  • Passing tone (PT): approached by step, left by step in the same direction.
  • Neighbor tone (NT): approached by step, left by step in the opposite direction.
  • Appoggiatura (APP): approached by leap, resolved by step; typically accented.
  • Suspension: prepared as a chord tone, held while the chord changes, resolved down by step; always accented.
  • Escape tone: approached by step, left by leap.
  • Anticipation: arrives before the chord it belongs to.
  • Pedal tone: sustained while harmonies change above it.

12.2 Prompt Implementation of Melodic Embellishment

Passing tones (smooth, scalar):

Style: "Folk, 90 BPM, stepwise melodies with passing tones, fingerpicked guitar, smooth and flowing"

Neighbor tones (ornamental):

Style: "Baroque, 100 BPM, ornamental neighbor tones in the melody, harpsichord, strings"

Appoggiaturas (dramatic, accented dissonance):

Style: "Romantic art song, 72 BPM, accented appoggiaturas resolving by step, piano and voice, expressive"

Suspensions (yearning, delayed resolution):

Style: "Choral, 68 BPM, 4–3 and 7–6 suspensions at cadences, a cappella choir, Renaissance style"

Pedal tone (static bass under motion):

Style: "Post-rock, 85 BPM, dominant pedal tone in the bass, changing harmonies above, building tension"

12.3 The NCT Diagnostic

Test: Write two prompts for the same harmonic progression. In Prompt A, specify "smooth stepwise melodies, passing tones, no accented dissonance." In Prompt B, specify "accented appoggiaturas, suspensions, dramatic leaps." Generate both and analyze the melodic interval profile and the placement of dissonance. The lesson: NCTs are the difference between a chord progression and a melody.
