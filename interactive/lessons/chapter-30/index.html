Chapter 30: Udio

30.1 Core Syntax

  • Style prompt: Prose-heavy description of genre, artist influence, production specifics, key, BPM, harmonic vocabulary
  • Exclusions: Minus-sign syntax (-electronic drums)
  • Style weight: Adjustable adherence to the style prompt
  • Workflow: Section-by-section generation, extend, inpaint

Udio is known for sound quality and surgical edits, with explicit key and time-signature tags and less trial-and-error than Suno.

30.2 Comprehensive Udio Prompt Examples

Example 1 — Indie-folk:

"intimate indie-folk, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, close-miked female vocals, muted piano, brushed drums, cello, warm analog production, key of D major, 88 BPM, bittersweet but hopeful, Bon Iver and Phoebe Bridgers influence"

Example 2 — Electronic:

"melodic techno, driving 4/4 kick, sidechained pad, plucked lead, dark atmospheric textures, key of A minor, 124 BPM, Afterlife and Tale of Us influence, Berlin club sound"

Example 3 — Orchestral:

"romantic orchestral, full string section, woodwinds, French horn melody, timpani rolls, key of C minor, 66 BPM, long crescendo from solo oboe to full orchestra, film-score production"

Example 4 — Jazz:

"bebop-influenced jazz trio, piano comping, walking upright bass, ride cymbal, key of B-flat major, 180 BPM, swing feel, Blue Note Records production, live room sound"

Example 5 — World music:

"West African Afrobeat, horn section, talking drum, electric guitar, call-and-response vocals, key of E minor, 105 BPM, Fela Kuti influence, live band production"

30.3 When to Prefer Udio

Udio rewards detailed prose over tag lists. A prompt that describes the harmonic behavior ("strings in pianissimo, harp arpeggiating, horns and clarinets introduce the theme") is more effective than a list of instruments. The professional learns to translate the ARR-SPEC into prose for Udio and into bracketed syntax for Suno.

Udio's specific strengths are sound quality and surgical edits. It exposes explicit key and time-signature tags, and its workflow involves less trial-and-error than Suno because the prompt is interpreted more literally. When the specification is primarily textural (orchestral, ambient, cinematic) rather than harmonic, Udio is often the better tool; when the specification is primarily chordal and sectional (pop, rock, jazz), Suno is often the better tool.
