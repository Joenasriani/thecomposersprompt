Chapter 10: The Theory 101 Prompt Template

10.1 The Four-Layer Template

[Key + Mode] → Style box: "in E minor" [Tempo + Meter] → "85 BPM, 4/4" [Chord Progression] → Bracketed chords in lyrics: [Em] [C] [G] [D] [Instrumentation + Texture] → "fingerpicked acoustic guitar, soft brushed drums, warm bass"

10.2 Six Copy-Ready Examples Across Genres

Example 1 — Indie-pop:

Style: Melancholic indie-pop, acoustic guitar, 90 BPM, warm male vocals, A minor key Lyrics: [Verse 1] [Am] City sleeps and [F] I am wide awake [C] Streetlights at the [G] window tremble soft [Chorus] [Am] Call me up now, [F] call me back [C] Through the years and [G] through the days

Example 2 — Country:

Style: Country, 92 BPM, G major key, acoustic guitar, pedal steel, fiddle, male vocals Lyrics: [Verse] [G] Dust on the [C] dashboard [G] Radio [D] low [Chorus] [G] Take me [C] home [G] Where the [D] rivers [G] flow

Example 3 — R&B:

Style: Contemporary R&B, 78 BPM, E♭ major key, smooth electric piano, finger snaps, layered female vocals Lyrics: [Verse] [E♭maj7] … [Cm7] … [A♭maj7] … [B♭7] [Chorus] [A♭maj7] … [B♭7] … [Gm7] … [Cm7]

Example 4 — Metal:

Style: Melodic death metal, 180 BPM, D minor key, down-tuned guitars, blast beats, growls and clean vocals Lyrics: [Verse] [Dm] … [B♭] … [F] … [C] [Chorus] [B♭] … [C] … [Dm] … [Dm]

Example 5 — Reggae:

Style: Roots reggae, 72 BPM, A minor key, skank guitar, one-drop drums, deep bass, male vocals Lyrics: [Verse] [Am] … [F] … [C] … [G] [Chorus] [F] … [G] … [Am] … [Am]

Example 6 — Classical:

Style: Classical string quartet, 96 BPM, C minor key, sonata form, violin, viola, cello Lyrics: [Exposition] [Cm] … [Fm] … [G7] … [Cm] [Development] [A♭] … [E♭] … [B♭] … [G7] [Recapitulation] [Cm] … [Fm] … [G7] … [Cm]

Assessment: Does the model stay in the key? Does it follow the bracketed chords? Which parameter was most stable? This is a lesson in model behavior as harmonic evidence.

10.3 The Berklee Foundation Quote

Berklee's Level 2 curriculum explicitly builds on foundations that the student is now ready for: "advanced harmony, voice leading, modes, chord extensions, modulation, and melodic construction." In AI terms, this is the vocabulary for sophistication. The Theory 101 template is the entry point; the chapters that follow are the ascent.

Part III: Level 2 — The Line and the Phrase

Level 1 specified single units: one interval, one mode, one key, one progression, one tempo, one contour. Level 2 adds a new grain of precision: the phrase. The student now specifies not just what chords to use but what harmonic job each section performs, where the cadence falls, and how the melodic line is embellished by non-chord tones. The level culminates in the ARR-SPEC method, which treats the section map as a theory exercise and compiles it into a prompt.
