Secondary Dominants

Temporarily tonicize a diatonic chord with its own dominant. Hear the leading-tone pull without confusing tonicization with modulation.

01 · CONSTRUCT AND COMPARE

Secondary Dominants in context

A secondary dominant is a dominant-function chord directed at a degree other than the primary tonic. In C major, A7 (A–C♯–E–G) is V7/ii because it points to D minor (ii). C♯ is the chromatic leading tone toward D. In conventional voice leading, C♯ tends to resolve upward to D and the seventh G tends to resolve downward to F.

Contrast ordinary vi–ii (A minor to D minor) with V7/ii–ii (A7 to D minor). One local tonicization does not necessarily establish a new key; the broader harmonic and melodic context determines whether modulation occurs.
