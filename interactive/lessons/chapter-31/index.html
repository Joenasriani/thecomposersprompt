Chapter 31: Translation Between Tools

31.1 The Translation Exercise

The same harmonic specification must be rendered in Suno syntax (bracketed chords, section production notes) and Udio syntax (comma-separated style tags, key/BPM at the end). The translation act reveals which aspects of the theory are structural (they survive re-encoding) and which are syntactic (they depend on a particular tool's parsing).

Specification Suno Rendering Udio Rendering
Key + mode A minor key in Style box key of A minor at end of prompt
Progression Bracketed chords across lyric lines i-VI-III-VII progression in style tags
Extension [Am9], [Cmaj7♯11] maj9 and m11 voicings
Modal interchange ♭VI borrowed from A minor in production notes modal interchange from parallel minor
Voicing Gm11 root position 1–♭3–5–♭7–9–11 close voicings, spread voicing indicated
Arrangement [Verse – piano only] section annotation verse is just piano, chorus adds full band
Negative constraints negative_tags: "no distortion" -distorted guitars

31.2 Worked Translation Example

Original specification:

"Dorian jazz-funk, 110 BPM, D Dorian, extended chords, clavinet, wah guitar, drums, bass"

Suno translation:

Style: "Jazz-funk, 110 BPM, D Dorian mode, extended chords, clavinet, wah guitar, tight drums, funk bass" Lyrics: [Dm9] … [G13] … [Dm9] … [G13]

Udio translation:

"jazz-funk in the style of Herbie Hancock, clavinet, wah guitar, tight drums, funk bass, key of D Dorian, extended and altered chord voicings, 110 BPM, 1970s fusion production"

AIVA translation:

D Dorian, I7-IV7 vamp, 110 BPM, funk

The AIVA version uses Roman numerals rather than chord letters, because AIVA's parser generates faster and more accurately from numerals.

31.3 Structural vs. Syntactic Knowledge

The translation act reveals which aspects of the theory are structural (they survive re-encoding) and which are syntactic (they depend on a particular tool's parsing). The musician who can say what they want in the language of harmony, and how to say it in the language of a given model, has achieved the dual literacy this curriculum aims to build.
