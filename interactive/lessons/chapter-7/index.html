Modal Interchange

Borrow chords from a parallel mode while retaining one tonal center. Hear how a single borrowed sonority changes the harmonic color.

01 · CONSTRUCT AND COMPARE

Modal Interchange in context

In C major, modal interchange can borrow harmonies from C minor without permanently changing the tonic. F minor (F–A♭–C) is the borrowed minor subdominant (iv); F major (F–A–C) is the diatonic major subdominant (IV). The borrowed A♭ creates chromatic coloration. This is not automatically a modulation: tonal center and phrase context determine the analysis.

Compare IV–I with iv–I in C major. The chromatic A♭ in F minor can descend to G in C major. Its expressive quality is contextual rather than guaranteed.
