Chapter 25: Inpainting, Stems, and the DAW

25.1 Section Replacement

Inpainting example:

"The second chorus works, but the last lyric line is wrong. Keep the preceding verse, chorus melody, and overall mood. Replace only that line."

Harmonic inpainting example:

"The bridge should modulate to the relative minor. Keep everything else the same, but replace the bridge's chord progression with Bm–G–D–A and adjust the melody to fit."

Melodic inpainting example:

"The chorus melody should rise higher on the word 'home.' Keep the harmony and rhythm the same, but raise the vocal line by a third."

25.2 Stem Separation

Stem separation (up to 12 stems on Suno's Auto Split, with Advanced Split selecting from nearly 100 instruments) enables export to a DAW for EQ, compression, automation, and mastering.

Stem-based workflow example:

  1. Generate full track
  2. Separate into stems: vocals, drums, bass, guitar, keys, strings
  3. Export to DAW
  4. Apply EQ to each stem
  5. Compress drums and bass
  6. Add reverb to vocals
  7. Automate volume on strings for dynamic shaping
  8. Master the mix

25.3 The Capstone Diagnostic

Full pipeline exercise:

Produce a complete ARR-SPEC for a three-minute composition with a tonal plan, a bar-accurate section map, an energy curve, and orchestration entries. Compile it to both a Suno prompt and an Udio prompt. Generate a batch, cull, and select the best candidate. Export stems to a DAW, apply the specified mix moves, and compare the final master against the original specification field by field.

Failure analysis template:

  • Constraints that survived: [list]
  • Constraints lost at prompt stage: [list + reason]
  • Constraints lost at generation stage: [list + reason]
  • Constraints recoverable in the mix: [list + technique]
  • Theoretical lessons: [what the gaps revealed about the harmonic concept]
