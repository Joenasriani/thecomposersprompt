CHAPTER 6.5 / MUSIC THEORY AND HARMONY

From scales to chords

A chord begins with notes. Learn how to build a major or minor triad, then hear the difference by changing just one pitch.

01 · CONSTRUCT

Build a chord from a scale

The C-major scale contains C D E F G A B. Start on C and select every other note: C–E–G. These are the root, major third and perfect fifth.

Change only E to E♭ and the chord becomes C minor. The root and perfect fifth stay the same.

Major third: E is 4 semitones above C. The perfect fifth G is 7 semitones above C.

Seven triads from C major

05 · ROOT, BASS AND INVERSION

Same chord; a different bass

The root names the chord; the bass is the lowest sounding pitch. A C-major chord retains C–E–G pitch classes in all three positions. C/E puts E in the bass; C/G puts G in the bass.

Root position: C4 · E4 · G4

06 · SEVENTH CHORDS

Adding one more chord tone

A seventh chord includes a fourth pitch. The symbol matters: Cmaj7 contains B, C7 contains B♭, and Cm7 changes E to E♭ as well as adding B♭. G7 in C major contains G–B–D–F and is the primary dominant seventh (V7).

07 · HARMONIC PROGRESSION

Theme A: eight bars

In C major, the eight-bar harmonic framework is C | G | Am | F | C | F | G | C, corresponding to I | V | vi | IV | I | IV | V | I. In 4/4 at quarter note = 96 BPM, one chord per bar gives 32 quarter-note beats and a nominal 20 seconds. These labels do not by themselves establish a performed melody or cadence.

Bar 1 · I · C major: C4 · E4 · G4

Bars 7–8 give V→I harmony, a possible authentic-cadence basis only when phrase context, rhythm and melody support closure.
