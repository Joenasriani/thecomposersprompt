28.5.1 The Harmony Decision Record

Adapted from Jack Righteous's production intelligence framework, the Harmony Decision Record is a structured diagnostic workflow for any AI-generated passage:

  1. Freeze a short baseline: verse, chorus, 8 bars.
  2. Find home: identify strongest stability.
  3. Name the audible problem: "chords feel static" / "chorus never arrives" / "melody sounds wrong."
  4. Identify harmonic job: stability, departure, expectation, bass movement, voicing.
  5. Change ONE variable: one substitution, bass inversion, harmonic rhythm change.
  6. Predict → A/B → Decide.

This builds the skill Berklee's new course aims for: "Develop a critical framework for assessing effectiveness, quality, and appropriate use of AI tools."

28.5.2 The Five-Step Debugging Protocol

From functional harmony research, the debugging protocol for any AI-generated harmonic passage:

  1. Identify implied key/mode.
  2. Map Roman numerals.
  3. Trace bass line (descending fifths = functional).
  4. Check voice-leading in close position (resolve 7ths down, leading tones up).
  5. Test cadential punctuation (does bar 4/8 land on I, IV, vi? Or on iii/vii°?).

This turns AI from composer to sparring partner.

28.5.3 The Prompt Library as Personal Theory Book

Berklee Online's AI for Music and Audio course syllabus shows the future: Lesson 1 Music Representations (sheet, symbolic, audio, spectrogram), Lesson 2 Extracting Pitch/Melody/Harmony/Chord Recognition, Lesson 4 Generative AI + Levels of Control + Copyright.

Students should maintain:

  • 10 prompts that reliably produce Dorian vs Aeolian
  • 5 prompts that fix a "random harmony" problem (reduce vocabulary, strengthen arrival)
  • 3 prompts where they forced modal stability via MIDI preconditioning

The portfolio is not a collection of "best prompts" but a record of the student's theory development, indexed by the failures that produced each revision.
