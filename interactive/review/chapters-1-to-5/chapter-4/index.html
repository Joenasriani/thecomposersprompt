Chapter 4: The Emotional Grammar of Intervals

4.1 Consonance, Dissonance, and the Affective Split

Intervals can be compared by their pitch distance and by how they sound in context. Consonance and dissonance are useful listening concepts, but they do not assign a universal emotion to each interval. Register, timbre, harmony, listening experience and musical setting all matter.

Chapter 4: The Emotional Grammar of Intervals

4.1 Consonance, Dissonance, and the Affective Split

Research on interval perception converges on a striking finding: when listeners map musical intervals to emotional and sensory qualities, nearly all the variance is accounted for by a single axis—consonance versus dissonance. Consonant intervals are consistently perceived as lighter, happier, more pleasant, softer, more tender, more relaxing, and smoother. Dissonant intervals are perceived as bigger, heavier, sadder, more unpleasant, tougher, more dominant, more arousing, harder, rougher, colder, and darker. A second dimension corresponds to interval size, independent of consonance—a major 6th and a minor 6th are both consonant, but they differ in perceived width and brightness.

This is not a matter of cultural convention alone. The association between consonant intervals and happiness/hopefulness, and between dissonant intervals and sadness/tragedy, has been demonstrated cross-modally: dissonance is mediated through perceived roughness, brightness, and warmth of color, and even brief explanations of consonance/dissonance shift listeners' ratings toward more dissonant, sadder, and more tragic judgments. Interval structure plays a critical role in the understanding of emotional musical meaning.

For the composer, this means that intervals are not abstract ratios; they are affective primitives—the smallest units of musical feeling. A minor 2nd is not just "one semitone"; it is a feeling of claustrophobia, dread, or scraping. A perfect 5th is not just "seven semitones"; it is a feeling of openness, stability, and power. The composer who can name these feelings can specify them in a prompt.
